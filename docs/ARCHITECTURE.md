# Stadium Kit — Architecture

The stadium kit is the asset side of a football-club game in which the player owns a stadium and
keeps rebuilding it. This repository produces the 3D assets and the rules for how they fit
together. The game UI, crowds, match engine and ad revenue live elsewhere and come later.

## Principles

1. **Blender is the factory, not the runtime.** Every asset is generated by Python run headless
   (`blender -b -P ...`). Nothing is hand-modelled, so every variant is a parameter, every build is
   reproducible, and every asset can be tested.
2. **Real specifications first.** Dimensions come from the Laws of the Game (IFAB), FIFA/UEFA
   stadium guidance and the UK Guide to Safety at Sports Grounds ("Green Guide"). Sources and
   numbers are in `docs/SPECS.md`. An asset that is the wrong size never looks real.
3. **Realism comes from physics and imperfection.** Nets are draped by cloth simulation, flags
   hang under gravity, mowing stripes are view-dependent (as on a real pitch), goalmouths wear,
   posts carry grass stains. Clean CG is what gives a render away.
4. **Engine-neutral.** Output is glTF 2.0 (`.glb`) plus JSON. Sockets, tint slots and metadata
   travel inside the glTF as node names and `extras`, so Godot, Unity, Unreal or the Rust demo
   can all read them.

## Coordinate conventions

- Metres. Blender is Z-up; glTF export converts to Y-up.
- The stadium origin is the centre mark. The pitch length runs along **X** (goals at ±X), the
  width along **Y**. "North" is +Y, "east" is +X.
- Every asset's origin is its attachment point on the ground. **Local +Y faces the pitch
  centre** (for a goal, the net extends towards −Y).

## Sockets: how pieces snap together

A socket is an empty named `SOCKET:<kind>:<id>` with custom properties (exported as glTF
`extras`):

| property      | meaning                                                     |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| `socket_kind` | what may attach (`goal`, `corner_flag`, `seat`, `stand_front`, …) |
| `socket_id`   | unique within its asset (`east`, `NE`, `row12_seat3`, …)    |
| other keys    | constraints, e.g. `max_width`, `riser_height`               |

Each asset root carries `asset_id`, `asset_family`, `fits` (the socket kind it attaches to) and
`params` (the JSON it was built from). Assembly is one rule: **put the asset's origin on the
socket's transform**. Any goal fits any `goal` socket, any seat fits any `seat` socket; that
standard interface is what makes everything snap and look right together.

Hierarchy of attachment (planned):

```
pitch ──► goal, corner_flag, dugout, ad_board_run, technical_area
  └─► perimeter ring ──► stand bays (straight / corner) ──► seats, stairs, vomitories,
                              │                              rails, fascia (ads/LED)
                              └─► roof modules ──► floodlights, gantries, screens
```

## Customisation: tint slots and variants

- **Variants** (goal style, seat model, grass type) are different builds of the same family,
  listed in `catalog/*.json`.
- **Tints** (club colours, net colours) do not need a rebuild. Any material whose name starts
  with `TINT_` is meant to be recoloured by the game at runtime. The slot name says what it is
  (`TINT_frame`, `TINT_net_a`, `TINT_flag`).
- Patterned surfaces (flags, later ad boards and seat mosaics) use generated image textures,
  so the pattern survives glTF export.

## Stand system

Stands are **generated, not modelled**, by `kit/stands/` (pure Python + numpy, no Blender). The game
needs this generator at runtime because players rebuild stands, so it is written to be ported
(Rust/GDScript) as-is. A whole 150k bowl generates in about 1.5 s.

1. **Sightlines** (`sightlines.py`). Each tier's row heights are solved from the C-value
   (`R = (N + C)(D + T)/D - N`, Green Guide), rounded up to 5 mm risers, capped at a 34 deg rake.
   Upper tiers stack on the tier below (overhang, clearance). If a requested overhang would leave
   any row below C = 60 mm, the solver steps the tier back until it doesn't, as a designer would.
2. **Runs and bays** (`geometry.py`). The stand front line is the pitch run-off plus `front_gap`.
   Each side is a straight *run*; with `corners: curved` the corners are arc runs and the bowl
   wraps continuously (all four sides must then share one profile). Runs are split into ~8 m bays
   (sized to fit exactly): half an aisle at each end, so neighbouring bays form 1 m aisles.
3. **Sections swept along runs.** Each bay of each tier is a few closed prisms: the seating block,
   two half-aisles with intermediate steps (risers <= 190 mm), and in every third bay a vomitory
   (tunnel mouth with 2.2 m headroom, side and front rails). Upper tiers get raking beams; a
   concourse block fills under them. Straight and curved runs use the same section, which is
   why corners join seamlessly.
4. **Anchors.** Every seat is an anchor on the back of its tread (position, yaw, row, tier, bay,
   actual pitch). Seats are never unique geometry.
5. **Sockets** emitted per bay: `fascia` (front of each tier, for LED ribbons and ads) and
   `roof_anchor` (back of the top tier, for roofs). Phases 3 and 4 attach to these.

Seats (`kit/assets/seat.py`, `catalog/seats.json`): six styles, each with variants chosen per
position (row-end standards, a bench bracket every fourth seat, a rail-seat post every second).
A seat's origin is the back of the tread, +Y to the pitch; each style declares its nominal width
and the minimum row depth that leaves a 305 mm seatway (stands warn if a tier is too shallow).

Seat colours (`kit/stands/patterns.py`): solid, tiers, rows, bays, gradient or random mix, plus
text/image mosaics (5x7 font, sized to fit, mirrored to read from the pitch, kept clear of
vomitories), then per-seat manufacturing variation and optional UV fade.

**Export.** Blender's glTF exporter cannot instance geometry-node instances, so
`tools/export_stadium.py` ships one mesh per seat variant and writes
`EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` arrays itself (`TRANSLATION`, `ROTATION`, `SCALE`, `_SEAT_COLOR`).
A 153k-seat bowl is 84 MB and a handful of draw calls; a test round-trips every seat.

## Roofs, facades and floodlights

Also generated per run, in pure numpy (`kit/stands/roofs.py`, `facades.py`, `floodlights.py`), with
thin Blender wrappers for materials and lights (`kit/assets/roof.py`, `facade.py`, `floodlight.py`).

- **Roofs.** `cantilever`: tapered Warren trusses on every bay boundary, a back column and a tied
  back-span, purlins and profiled sheeting (with a translucent leading strip). It rises toward the
  pitch and is capped at 60 m. `goalpost`: a front girder (truss, optionally clad as the classic
  fascia) on posts standing in the front rows, with rafters back to the rear wall. With posts in
  every bay it is the small covered stand. The solver raises the leading edge until (a) the back
  row can see a ball 15 m above the far touchline and (b) every covered tread keeps 2.5 m of
  headroom. Curved runs give a continuous ring roof. Sockets: `roof_edge` per bay.
- **Facades** sit behind the deepest structure *and* the roof's back-span tie, so the roof
  structure is enclosed. Six systems: `concrete_frame`, `brick`, `metal_bands`, `glass`, `etfe`
  (bulging diamond cushions that can glow at night), `fins`. Plus ground-floor entrances, stair
  cores, gable walls on exposed stand ends, and a paved plaza. Sockets: `facade_sign` per bay.
- **Floodlights.** `corner_pylons` (lattice or tube masts on the diagonals, lamps at least 25 deg
  above the centre spot), `roof` (an LED ribbon on the roof edge) and `poles` (grassroots). Every
  fixture is aimed by a greedy optimiser, as lighting designers do: it repeatedly re-aims the
  fixture that most improves Emin/Emax at the darkest point. Lamp power is then set so the
  analytically computed average hits the lux target. Elite designs meet UEFA uniformity
  (U1 >= 0.7, U2 >= 0.8); grassroots designs meet EN 12193 class III (U2 >= 0.5). At night, spot
  lights are placed per fixture, with house lights under the roof for the seating bowl. A Cycles
  render of a 2000-lux design lands within 11% of a 2000-lux reference sun.

## Advertising, screens, dugouts and tunnel

Placement maths is pure numpy (`kit/ads/placement.py`); Blender wrappers live in
`kit/assets/ads.py`, `screen.py`, `dugout.py` and `tunnel.py`.

- **Creatives.** `kit/ads/studio.py` renders board artwork and the scoreboard with Workbench in a
  scene of its own, so every ad is a real texture that exports to glTF. Brands are fictional
  (`catalog/brands.json`). LED content is a horizontal atlas of the layout's chosen sponsors.
- **Perimeter boards** run 4 m behind the touchlines and 5.5 m behind the goal lines, with a gap on
  the main-stand side sized from the dugouts. `led`: a continuous housing with an emissive face
  (seen through an LED-pixel mask, one LED per texel); `static`: 7.2 m printed panels on A-frames.
- **3D carpets** are generated by projection. Each vertex of a virtual upright board is projected
  from the main TV camera onto the ground, so from that camera the carpet is indistinguishable
  from a standing board. The height shrinks if the footprint would reach the end boards.
- **LED ribbons** run along upper-tier fascias. **Screens**: hung under the roof edge at the ends,
  in the corners, as a centre-hung cube (about 25 m above the pitch, cabled to the roof), or on
  frames on top of stands without roofs.
- **Text direction.** Seen from the pitch, a run reads right-to-left, so every texture's u runs
  against the run direction (the same rule the seat mosaics use). Tests enforce this for boards
  and screens.
- **Dugouts** (shell / box / shelter) sit either side of halfway, 4.3 m back from the touchline,
  with dashed technical areas. **Tunnel**: a portal in the main stand front plus an optional
  telescopic tunnel to the dugout line.
- **Ad inventory.** Every placement is an `ad_slot` socket carrying its kind, area, brand and a TV
  weight (how squarely and how closely it faces the main camera, 0–1). Totals are summed per kind
  for the revenue model.

## Game export (`tools/export_stadium.py --game`)

The render path keeps everything procedural; the game path converts the assembled stadium into
plain glTF that any engine draws correctly (`kit/core/game.py`, `kit/core/bake.py`):

- **Materials.** Every procedural material becomes standard PBR. Large surfaces (concrete, brick,
  paving, roof sheeting, facade cladding) get tileable albedo/roughness/normal sets baked with
  Cycles from a lone plane in a scene of its own, made seamless by cross-fading with a half-offset
  copy (or baked as an exact multiple of the pattern for bricks and paving) and mapped in metres
  through KHR_texture_transform. `TINT_` surfaces get a greyscale detail texture times a colour
  factor plus `tint_gain` in extras, so engines recolour them at runtime. The pitch gets a
  full-surface albedo bake (wear and variation) plus a tiling blade normal; mowing stripes stay an
  engine shader (docs/SPECS.md). Small props get their measured average albedo and roughness.
  Bakes are cached by a fingerprint of each material's node values.
- **LODs.** Seats ship at LOD1 (18% of the hero mesh) with an LOD2 (5%) linked through
  `MSFT_lod`; the LOD nodes are detached from the scene graph so engines without the extension
  never draw them. Nets ship as `Net_LOD1` cards: the simulated net surface textured with a
  generated cord pattern (alpha mask), about 1.3% of the cord geometry.
- **Lights.** Floodlights export as `KHR_lights_punctual` spots (candela from the designed power).
  Bowl house lights, which glTF cannot express as area lights, become wide spots of equal intensity.
- **Templates** (models that exist only to be instanced) never ship.

`tools/preview_game.py` renders a game `.glb` imported into an empty Blender scene, and the Rust
viewer (`viewer/`) renders it in real time, so both prove what an engine actually receives.

## Layout file

A stadium is one JSON document (`schema: stadium-layout/1`), e.g. `layouts/sunday_league.json`.
The game saves and edits this; the assembler turns it into a scene. Sections:
`pitch`, `goals`, `corner_flags`, then (later) `perimeter`, `stands`, `roof`, `ads`, `lighting`.

## Game-readiness

- `.glb` per asset in `out/assets/`, plus `out/manifest.json` (ids, dims, sockets, triangle
  counts, tint slots).
- Procedural Blender shaders do not survive glTF export. Pitch grass, paint and wear are
  documented as portable shader maths in `docs/SPECS.md` (game engines re-implement them in a
  few lines), and other procedural materials will be baked to PBR texture sets in a later phase.
- Hero geometry (cord-level nets, grass blades) is for close-ups and renders. Game LODs (alpha-card
  nets, shader-only grass) come in the LOD phase.

## Testing

- `tests/run_tests.py` (inside Blender) checks dimensions against `SPECS.md`, socket presence and
  naming, tint-slot naming, triangle budgets, and that every `.glb` re-imports.
- `tools/review.py` renders each asset with Cycles under a physical sky from fixed review cameras
  (broadcast, close-up, macro) into `out/review/`. "Lifelike" is judged on these renders.

## Roadmap

1. **Pitch level:** pitch (grass types, mowing, lines, wear), goals, corner flags. *(done)*
2. **Stands:** six seat styles, sightline solver, bay generator, aisles, vomitories, curved
   corners, terraces with crush barriers, colour patterns and mosaics, instanced export. *(done)*
3. **Roofs, facades, floodlights:** two roof systems (5 presets), six facades, three floodlight
   installations designed to lux and uniformity targets, night lighting. *(done)*
4. **Matchday dressing:** generated ad creatives, LED and printed perimeter boards, projected 3D
   carpets, LED ribbons, four screen mountings, three dugout styles, technical areas, tunnel,
   ad inventory with TV weights. *(done)*
5. **Game-ready export:** baked PBR materials, seat and net LODs, exported lights, and a Rust
   real-time viewer with LOD switching and a floodlight irradiance volume. *(done)*
